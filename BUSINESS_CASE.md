## 1. Executive Summary

The Smart Energy & Saving System (SESS) is a cloud-connected, hardware-software solution that gives Indonesian homeowners complete visibility and control over their household electricity consumption. By combining a whole-house energy meter, room-by-room smart switching, solar autonomy tracking, and a smartphone app backed by cloud infrastructure, SESS enables families to reduce monthly electricity bills by an estimated 15–30% without changing their lifestyle.

Indonesia's residential electricity sector presents a clear, underserved opportunity: rising PLN tariffs, rapid solar panel adoption, and limited consumer-grade monitoring tools have created a gap that SESS is uniquely positioned to fill. This document outlines the market rationale, product differentiation, financial upside, and execution plan that support a focused investment in SESS commercialisation.

**The ask:** Seed-stage investment to fund hardware manufacturing at scale, distribution channel development, and marketing to reach 10,000 installed households within 24 months.

## 2. Problem Statement

### 2.1 Rising Energy Costs Without Visibility

Electricity is one of the largest and least controllable recurring expenses for Indonesian households. PLN tariffs have trended upward across multiple adjustment cycles, yet most households still receive a single monthly bill with no breakdown of which devices or rooms drive consumption. Homeowners pay without knowing — and cannot change what they cannot see.

### 2.2 Accidental Waste is Systemic

Air conditioners, water heaters, and idle appliances left running unintentionally account for a significant share of residential electricity use. Manual routines — checking switches before leaving the house, turning off each room individually — are error-prone and inconvenient. There is no low-effort way for a typical family to act on wasteful behaviour in real time.

### 2.3 Solar Adoption Without Optimisation

Indonesia's rooftop solar market is growing rapidly, driven by government net-metering schemes and falling panel costs. However, most solar adopters have no real-time view of how much they generate versus consume versus draw from the grid. Without this data, households cannot optimise appliance scheduling to maximise self-consumption and minimise grid dependency — leaving significant savings unrealised.

### 2.4 The Status Quo Fails Consumers

Existing tools — utility bills, basic smart plugs, and manual inspection — address at most one dimension of this problem and require either expert knowledge or manual effort. There is no integrated, consumer-friendly platform that combines monitoring, control, and solar tracking in a single product.

## 3. Proposed Solution

SESS is a three-layer system: smart hardware installed at the household, a cloud platform that aggregates data in real time, and a smartphone application that puts full control in the homeowner's hand.

### 3.1 Core Capabilities

| Capability | Customer Pain Point Addressed |
|---|---|
| **Whole-House Energy Meter** | Eliminates blind billing — shows live kW draw for the entire home |
| **Room-Based Device Control** | Stops accidental waste — switch any room on/off remotely |
| **Solar Autonomy Tracking** | Maximises solar ROI — live Generate / Consume / From Grid dashboard |
| **Cloud-Based Remote Access** | Delivers control from anywhere — full management via smartphone |

#### Whole-House Energy Meter
A single hardware unit installed at the main panel measures real-time power draw across the entire household and streams it to the cloud. Homeowners see live consumption at a glance — in kilowatts, not month-end statements — making it immediately actionable.

#### Room-Based Device Control
Smart relays installed at individual circuit breakers or wall outlets give per-room switching capability. Users can monitor which rooms are drawing power and toggle devices on or off from the app — whether they are in the next room or across the country.

#### Solar Autonomy Tracking
For the growing segment of homes with rooftop solar panels, SESS tracks three metrics simultaneously: energy generated by the panels, energy consumed by the household, and energy drawn from the PLN grid. The autonomy percentage — how much of a household's needs are met by its own generation — becomes a live KPI that motivates behaviour change and validates the solar investment.

#### Cloud-Based Remote Access
All data flows through a secure cloud platform, making the same interface available from any internet-connected smartphone. There is no local hub dependency; if the home has connectivity, the homeowner has control.

## 4. Target Market

### 4.1 Primary Segments

**Segment 1 — High-Bill Homeowners**  
Households spending IDR 500,000 or more per month on electricity. This group experiences direct financial pain and is most motivated by cost-reduction messaging. They are spread across urban and peri-urban areas of Java, Sumatra, and Kalimantan, where middle-class household electricity consumption is highest.

**Segment 2 — Solar Panel Adopters**  
Homeowners who have installed or are planning to install rooftop solar. These consumers have already made a capital investment in energy independence and are highly receptive to tools that maximise the return on that investment. Indonesia's installed residential solar base is growing at an estimated 20–30% annually.

**Segment 3 — Tech-Forward Households**  
Urban professionals and families aged 25–45 who have already adopted smart home devices (smart TVs, smart speakers, IP cameras). They are comfortable with app-based control and motivated by convenience and status as well as cost savings. This segment is typically the earliest adopter and strongest word-of-mouth channel.

### 4.2 Market Size Estimate

Indonesia has approximately 85 million household electricity subscribers. Targeting the middle-upper segment with monthly bills above IDR 500,000 gives an addressable population of roughly 15–20 million households. At an assumed average system price of IDR 3,000,000 (hardware + installation), the total addressable market (TAM) exceeds IDR 45 trillion. A realistic serviceable addressable market (SAM) for the next 3–5 years — constrained by distribution reach and brand awareness — is estimated at 500,000 households, representing a IDR 1.5 trillion revenue opportunity.

## 5. Competitive Landscape

### 5.1 Current Alternatives

| Alternative | Strength | Weakness vs. SESS |
|---|---|---|
| **PLN Monthly Bill** | Universal, no cost | Zero real-time visibility; no control capability |
| **Basic Smart Plugs** (Xiaomi, TP-Link) | Low unit cost, widely available | Single-device, no whole-house view, no solar tracking |
| **Utility Smart Meters** (PLN AMI rollout) | Government-backed infrastructure | Read-only; no control; solar tracking absent |
| **Premium Home Automation** (Schneider, ABB) | Enterprise-grade reliability | High cost (IDR 50M+); complex installation; not consumer-friendly |
| **Solar Inverter Apps** | Native solar data | Isolated to generation data only; no consumption or control |

### 5.2 SESS Differentiators

1. **Integrated view:** The only product that unifies consumption monitoring, room-level control, and solar autonomy in a single consumer app.
2. **Accessible price point:** Positioned between basic smart plugs and enterprise automation at a price achievable for the middle-market household.
3. **Indonesian market focus:** Designed for PLN tariff structures, the Indonesian solar net-metering framework, and local installation norms.
4. **Cloud-native architecture:** No local hub means no single point of hardware failure and seamless over-the-air updates.

## 7. Implementation & Go-to-Market Plan

### 7.1 Phase 1 — Product Validation (Months 1–6)

- Deploy pilot installations with 50–100 households across 3 cities (Jakarta, Surabaya, Bandung)
- Validate energy savings claims with measured before/after data
- Refine mobile app UX based on user feedback
- Establish hardware supply chain and quality benchmarks

### 7.2 Phase 2 — Channel Development (Months 6–18)

- Recruit and certify a network of 50+ electrical installers and smart-home integrators as authorised SESS partners
- List hardware through e-commerce platforms (Tokopedia, Shopee) for direct-to-consumer sales with self-installation option for tech-savvy buyers
- Partner with solar panel installers to bundle SESS as a recommended add-on at point of solar installation
- Run targeted digital marketing to high-bill and solar-adopter segments via social media and search

### 7.3 Phase 3 — Scale (Months 18–36)

- Expand installer network to 200+ certified partners nationally
- Introduce tiered subscription plans (Basic / Pro with advanced analytics and scheduling)
- Explore B2B channel: property developers and housing estates as bulk buyers for new builds
- Assess regional expansion to Malaysia, Vietnam, and the Philippines where similar market conditions apply

### 7.4 Customer Onboarding Flow

1. **Consultation** — Homeowner contacts SESS via WhatsApp or website; usage profile assessed
2. **Hardware installation** — Certified installer visits the home; whole-house meter and room relays fitted (2–4 hours)
3. **App setup** — Installer registers the household on the cloud platform; homeowner downloads the app and confirms connectivity
4. **Live monitoring begins** — Dashboard active from day one; savings visible within the first billing cycle

## 8. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hardware supply chain disruption** | Medium | High | Dual-source key components; maintain 3-month safety stock |
| **Data privacy and security concerns** | Medium | High | End-to-end encryption; local data processing option; transparent privacy policy; ISO 27001 target in Year 2 |
| **Consumer adoption friction** | High | Medium | WhatsApp-first consultation flow; self-installation kit for simpler homes; installer partnership reduces friction for mainstream buyers |
| **Competitive entry by large players** | Medium | High | Build switching costs via subscription data, installer loyalty programme, and deep solar integration before incumbents react |
| **Regulatory changes (PLN net-metering)** | Low | Medium | Monitor policy closely; product architecture supports multiple billing models |
| **Hardware reliability and warranty claims** | Low | High | Rigorous QA process; 12-month hardware warranty; remote diagnostics via cloud platform reduces field service costs |

## 9. Recommendation

SESS addresses a genuine, growing, and financially significant problem for Indonesian homeowners: paying too much for electricity they cannot see, control, or optimise. The product is technically differentiated, the market is large and underserved, and the economics — payback in under 20 months for a typical household — provide a compelling customer value proposition that makes selling straightforward.

The three key reasons to proceed:

1. **Market timing is right.** Rising electricity tariffs, solar adoption growth, and smartphone penetration have created a receptive audience that did not exist five years ago.
2. **The product is integrated where competitors are fragmented.** No current offering combines whole-house monitoring, room control, and solar autonomy at a consumer price point.
3. **Revenue diversification from day one.** Hardware sales fund near-term cash flow while a recurring subscription layer builds long-term enterprise value.

**Investment ask:** Seed funding to cover hardware manufacturing (Minimum Order Quantity for first production run), sales and marketing in Year 1, cloud infrastructure scale-up, and installer certification programme. Expected milestones: 1,000 installed households by end of Year 1, contribution break-even at 2,500 households, and a clear path to 15,000 households and IDR 34.5 B annual revenue by Year 3.

SESS is not a feature — it is an infrastructure layer for the smart, sustainable Indonesian home. The foundation is built. Now is the time to scale.
